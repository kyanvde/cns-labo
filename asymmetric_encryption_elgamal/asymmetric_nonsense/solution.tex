\section{Asymmetric Nonsense}

\challenge{The crypto people always with their nonce's, why does it matter so much.}

We are given an ElGamal public key $(p, g, y)$, a known plaintext $m$ with its
ciphertext $(c_1, c_2)$, and the encrypted flag $(\tilde{c}_1, \tilde{c}_2)$.

Both ciphertexts have the same first component, $c_1 = \tilde{c}_1$. Since
$c_1 = g^k \bmod p$, both messages were encrypted with the same nonce $k$, and
therefore with the same shared secret $s = y^k \bmod p$:
\begin{align*}
  c_2         &= m \cdot s \bmod p, \\
  \tilde{c}_2 &= f \cdot s \bmod p.
\end{align*}

Because we know $m$ (as a big-endian integer of its bytes), we can recover the
shared secret from the first ciphertext:
\[
  s = c_2 \cdot m^{-1} \bmod p.
\]

With the shared secret, the flag $f$ follows from the second ciphertext:
\[
  f = \tilde{c}_2 \cdot s^{-1} \bmod p.
\]
